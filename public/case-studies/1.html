01

The problem

K–12 teachers spend an average of 5-7 hours/week on lesson planning. According to my wife (a very talented high school teacher), the most tedious part of the process is finding supplemental resources; the perfect video, an additional image that hammers a point, a short quiz to fill the time. Our existing tool required manually sifting through over 6 million assets, that when searched, often provided little value, duplicated resources, or irrelevant results.

  • Our current search did not understand grade level, subject, or individual teacher needs
  • Teachers did not need 6 million assets; they needed 3-4 relevant, high-quality resources
  • Even when a relevant asset was found, you still had to manually integrate it into your lesson plan
Opportunity: Context-aware AI, that could save time and frustration.

simplified LLM asset retrieval workflow

02

Design principles

Contextual
The tool understands who the teacher is, and the results they want back, not a generic prompt box.
Efficent
Not designed to be a chat bot. Designed to be a tool that delivers a result, and allows adjustments.
Simple & Actionable
Reduces cognitive load, saves time, gives exceptional return.

05

Key UX decisions

Tone calibration
Professional, collegial voice — not the perky AI assistant tone teachers immediately distrust.
Error handling
When the AI was uncertain, it said so. Honesty over confidence. Trust built over time.
Follow-up prompts
Surfaced contextual next steps — "Differentiate for ELL students?" — reducing blank-page anxiety.
Editing affordance
Output was always editable inline. AI as co-author, not authority. Teachers stayed in control.

07

Reflection

Biggest surprise
Teachers used it most for differentiation — not our assumed primary use case. Research confirmed it; we hadn't fully designed for it.
Would redesign
Onboarding — context-setting felt like a form. It should feel like a conversation. The friction was unnecessary.
What's next
Persistent teacher profiles, curriculum alignment, and collaborative lesson planning across teams.
