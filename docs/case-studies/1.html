Research & discovery

Conducted [X] teacher interviews and workflow observations across [X] schools. Three insights shaped everything that followed.

  • Trust before speed — teachers distrust AI they can't verify; credibility is earned through transparency
  • Time pressure is acute — Sunday nights and planning periods are when the pain is sharpest
  • The blank page is the real problem — a starting point beats a polished draft

03

Design principles

Contextual
The tool understands who the teacher is and who their students are — not a generic prompt box.
Transparent
AI shows its reasoning. Errors are surfaced, not hidden. Confidence builds over time.
Efficient
Reduces cognitive load. Doesn't create new tasks. Saves time where it matters most.

05

Key UX decisions

Tone calibration
Professional, collegial voice — not the perky AI assistant tone teachers immediately distrust.
Error handling
When the AI was uncertain, it said so. Honesty over confidence. Trust built over time.
Follow-up prompts
Surfaced contextual next steps — "Differentiate for ELL students?" — reducing blank-page anxiety.
Editing affordance
Output was always editable inline. AI as co-author, not authority. Teachers stayed in control.

07

Reflection

Biggest surprise
Teachers used it most for differentiation — not our assumed primary use case. Research confirmed it; we hadn't fully designed for it.
Would redesign
Onboarding — context-setting felt like a form. It should feel like a conversation. The friction was unnecessary.
What's next
Persistent teacher profiles, curriculum alignment, and collaborative lesson planning across teams.
