Helios performance reviews: question types, feedback, and company goals

01

The problem

Helios is an HR company, and performance reviews were the next product on its roadmap. It was a 0 → 1 build: no existing product to iterate on, and no single customer to design for.

The system had to serve three very different people at once, across many kinds of reviews, for companies of any size, in any industry, anywhere.

  • Admins configure review cycles, templates, and permissions for their whole company
  • Managers write reviews, calibrate across their team, and hold the conversation
  • Employees reflect on their own work, receive feedback, and track growth
  • Multiple review types: annual, quarterly, 360, upwards, self, probationary, and custom, each with different participants, questions, and response types
Opportunity: one flexible system, not a pile of one-off review forms.

04

Designing the system

Every review type is built from the same building blocks, so the question builder had to give managers real flexibility without making form creation a chore.

  • Flexible question setup: managers choose from multiple question and response types, such as single choice, multi-select, linear scales, and short answer
  • Inline WYSIWYG editing: questions are edited directly on the card, exactly as employees will see them
  • Easy form creation: add, reorder, and configure questions in a few clicks, with sensible defaults to start from

Question cards for each response type, edited inline

05

Launching a review campaign

Once templates exist, administrators turn them into a review campaign: a single, scheduled send-out to the people across the organization. The setup had to feel as light as sending a survey, while still handling the complexity of a whole company.

  • Choose the template: start from an admin-built template for the review type being run
  • Set the schedule: open and due dates, plus reminders for anyone still in progress
  • Select the audience: reach the organization by department, location, or individual people
  • Review and send: confirm who will receive it, then launch, and track completion from the dashboard

Very simple interactive prototype: click Continue (or a step in the header) to move through the four steps of campaign setup

06

Reviewing feedback

Once responses come in, managers need to make sense of feedback from many people at once. I designed one view per person that gathers every response, so the review conversation starts from a complete picture.

  • Filter by reviewer: see everything together, or narrow to a single reviewer
  • Results at a glance: choice questions show vote counts and percentages, with the reviewers behind them
  • Context on every comment: each written response is labeled with the reviewer's relationship, such as manager, peer, or upwards

Manager view of feedback for one employee, with reviewer filtering and relationship labels

07

Admin dashboard

Administrators run reviews for the whole organization, so they needed one place to see what is due, what is overdue, and how every campaign is progressing, without opening each review.

  • Due soon: upcoming reviews and who they belong to, surfaced first
  • Snapshot: average completion rate and active goals at a glance
  • Active campaigns: per-person status (completed, in progress, overdue, canceled) and a completion rate that can be broken down by department or country
  • Create new campaign: always one click away, leading into the setup flow above

Admin dashboard: due soon, snapshot metrics, and active campaign progress

08

Outcome

I left Helios before the product launched, so there are no usage metrics to share. What I can point to is the scale of what was designed.

14 end-to-end flows designed
3 jobs to be done
28 cross-platform touch points

Taking a complete performance review system from nothing to designed, from template creation and campaign launch to completing reviews, reviewing feedback, and admin reporting, was a tremendous undertaking for three months of work.

The work was considered a great success by the C-suite and our stakeholders.
