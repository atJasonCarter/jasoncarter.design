A flexible dashboard built for personalized role use

A single, configurable interface served ops managers, dispatchers, and executives

Role Lead Product Designer
Timeline 3 months · 2020
Platform Web application
Team 2 designers, 4 engineers

Final product: the logistics dashboard across user contexts

01

The problem

Multiple user types. Multiple mental models. One shared platform. Operations managers needed ground-level control, dispatchers needed real-time awareness, and executives needed a 30,000-foot view.

  • Existing tools were rigid and did not supply actionable insight
  • Critical data was buried under poor or no navigation; users built parallel workarounds in spreadsheets
  • No consistent design, or use of design systems
Opportunity: one flexible system, designed to be expanded, improved and adaptable

Existing state: Built for a specific user, no available actions, confusing navigation

02

Research & discovery

Conducted 8 interviews and shadowing sessions across all our user groups. We discovered we had vast ammounts of unstructured data that our internal users were developing their own hacky workarounds to get. Most had a tree of bookmarks to different json files, excel sheets etc. No one had everything they wanted at a glance. For example,

Operations managers
Need granular, actionable data. Heavily focused on tracking, and order details. Needed to catch problems in real time High dwell time.
Managers
Need news, insights, and financial information, total counts of shipments, etc.

research repository I designed and built in Notion

Discovery: stakeholder interviews across roles.

Key insight: flexibility couldn't mean complexity. We initially planned on having prebuilt layouts for each user role, but every user wanted unique setups. We leaned into it and focused on having a teachable empty state for first time use, and saved views tied to user.
03

Design principles

Individual, not role-locked
No one stuck with someone else's view. Fully customizable.
Full featured
Sort widgets by use, but let users find the tools they need to do their job.
Instructional
Customization must be obvious and immediate, users should learn they have access to more than they knew.

initial lofi wireframes

04

Design decisions

Empty state

Design detail: hover tooltips showed previews of the widget content

Shipments, with ability to bookmark specific shipments, tab views, and for the first time, live tracking.

Layout customization: snap-to grid, utilizing jQuery, everything can be resized, moved, or deleted

05

Outcomes

Users stopped maintaining their parallel spreadsheets. They closed tabs. When people trust a dashboard, they stop building workarounds.

"We're going to be able to build so much faster now that we have a design system in place, and design logic to build off of"

— Josh, Engineer, on 'Unintended' Consequences

"I love how easy this is, I have so many open tabs I can close now! I honestly get so frustrated nearly every day when im in a call and im trying to find the right tab and now it's all just...here"

— Steph, Operations Manager, on Product Fit

Dashboard in production, rapid, sticky adoption

Abandoned workarounds = the real adoption signal
06

Reflection

Biggest surprise
How quickly people learned to use and edit. We had several weeks of discussion that we might have to hold trainings, and rethink the onboarding. Learned to trust users.
Would redesign
The design system. We were instructed to use material design components, but they didn't fit our specific use cases. At the time, I wasn't a good enough communicator to know how to push back.
What's next
Mobile views, better aesthetics, tab-pages (e.g. shipments, financials, news)
